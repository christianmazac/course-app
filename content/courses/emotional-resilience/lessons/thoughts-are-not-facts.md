# Make room between thought and fact

Under pressure, a thought can sound like a certain prediction: “I always get this wrong.” Treating a thought as information to examine—not a fact you must obey—can make more choices visible.

## Try the three-column note

| What happened (observable) | The story my mind added | Another possible explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A message has no reply yet | “They are upset with me” | They may be busy; I do not know yet |

Do not force a positive interpretation. The aim is to separate what you know from what you are guessing and to make room for more than one explanation.

## Choose a useful response

Ask what you would do next if you held the thought lightly. You might wait, ask a clarifying question, or take care of something else while you learn more.

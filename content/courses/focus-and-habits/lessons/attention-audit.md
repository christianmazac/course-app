# Notice where your attention goes

Attention is shaped by your surroundings, energy, and the tasks in front of you. A short observation is more useful than blaming yourself for distraction.

## A two-day attention audit

Three times a day, jot down what you were trying to do, what pulled your attention away, and how you were feeling. Keep the notes factual: “notifications appeared” is more actionable than “I have no discipline.”

After two days, look for one repeating cue. Perhaps a noisy room, an unclear next step, or a low-energy time is making focus harder.

## Choose one adjustment

Change one condition for a single work session: silence notifications, write the next action on paper, or take a short break first. Notice whether the adjustment helped. The aim is useful information, not a perfect record.
